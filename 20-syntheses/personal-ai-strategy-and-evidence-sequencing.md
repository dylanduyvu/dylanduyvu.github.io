# Personal AI strategy and evidence sequencing

## Bottom Line

Niyant is not using a narrow enterprise workflow to scope the first experiment. He starts from a broad problem and solution hypothesis: businesses spend substantial human effort producing and transmitting context to LLMs, and a live collection and learning system may reduce that burden.

The immediate plan is a static self-demo on available personal data. Its practical job is to show technical ability, increase legitimacy, and attract inbound. Enterprise workflow discovery comes later. Outbound is a fallback if public demos, writing, the landing page, the pricing page, and other inbound strategies fail.

That sequence is coherent, but each stage produces a different kind of evidence. The stages should not be allowed to borrow credibility from one another.

## What the Slack exchange changed

### The formal program is not the near-term build

The public Phase 1 document describes reconstruction, next-action prediction, source ablations, continual updates, prospective repetition, controlled suggestion exposure, and real outcome tests. Niyant said he does not plan to implement roughly 80% of it now. The near-term artifact is a static implementation with basic data.

The formal document remains a useful roadmap. The static demo should not be reported as completion of Phase 1.

### The first data sources come from availability

The experiment begins with Obsidian note-taking and then browser use because Niyant has those data. That is a reasonable way to make the first build tractable. It is not evidence that note-taking or browser context is the best enterprise wedge.

### The enterprise thesis is an investment filter

Niyant said the point of finding a growing enterprise problem is to have a reason to do the work at all. He considers the broad context-supply thesis to be the current problem LBH and largely complete once he cleans up comments.

This means the LBH is currently being resolved through thesis construction rather than customer validation. It can establish a plausible reason to investigate. It cannot establish pain frequency, budget, buyer, deployment tolerance, or willingness to pay.

### Public proof is a legitimacy and inbound strategy

There is no Phase 1 experiment that automatically triggers enterprise outreach. Niyant intends to build legitimacy incrementally through public demos, a pricing page, a landing page, and blogs, then collect inbound. He would initiate outbound if that set of inbound tactics fails.

Public proof may create access. Access is not the same as product fit.

### Computer-use NAP is now an assigned complementary workstream

On July 22, Dylan asked what Niyant would have him, or a second pair of hands generally, build to contribute to the vision. Dylan reported that Niyant's answer was to build the computer-use NAP concept. This resolves whether the direction is strategically inside the vision, but not the exact prediction target or implementation.

The clean division is currently:

- Niyant continues the static next-write and personal-context comparison on Obsidian and browser data.
- Dylan tests whether the next exact semantic computer destination is diverse, predictable beyond trivial habits, and valuable enough for a Tab-style routing interface.

Omar Shaikh's Tada and Tabracadabra are prior art for a context-aware writer after the user has already selected a field. Dylan decided to skip a separate Tabracadabra trial and go directly to the distinct routing test. That test should remain offline and navigation-only until it beats recency, transition, screen-only, and mismatched-history controls.

## What each stage can show

| Stage | It may show | It does not show |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise thesis | The problem and proposed solution are coherent enough to investigate | The cost estimate is correct, urgent, budgeted, or owned by a specific buyer |
| Static personal demo | A narrow implementation works on Niyant's available data under stated baselines | Dynamic ingestion, continual learning, enterprise transfer, or business value |
| Public artifacts | Builder quality, seriousness, clarity, and perhaps technical novelty | Enterprise demand or willingness to deploy |
| Inbound interest | Some people found the work relevant enough to engage | The product solves a recurring workflow problem or deserves a budget |
| Enterprise workflow test | The pipeline can reconstruct useful context inside one real work process | Broad horizontal fit |
| Controlled outcome experiment | The system caused an improvement on a defined outcome | General autonomy or the later phases of the thesis |

## The three outputs of the demo

The demo can produce three different outputs:

1. Proof of builder quality: Niyant can frame and execute a rigorous public project.
2. Proof of technical efficacy: the implementation beats appropriate baselines on a specific task.
3. Proof of market demand: an enterprise will adopt, retain, and pay for the solution.

The first can survive an honest failed experiment. It should not be reported as the second or third.

## Load-bearing tensions

- The proposed product emphasizes dynamic ingestion and continual learning, while the current build is static. The subset under test must be named explicitly.
- The personal and enterprise MVP may share collection and cleaning code, but shared code does not establish shared workflow fit.
- The broad thesis may be the correct abstraction for a long-term research program while still being too broad for sales or deployment.
- Inbound-first avoids premature cold outreach, but it needs a failure condition. Otherwise outbound can be deferred indefinitely.
- A technically impressive proof may be necessary to earn enterprise attention. That does not mean the most impressive proof is the cheapest or most informative next experiment.

## Open Questions

- What minimum static result would change belief in the dynamic system rather than merely show that the pipeline runs?
- Which claim from formal Phase 1 is the first demo actually attempting to falsify?
- How will Niyant distinguish context that supplies facts from context that specifies desires, judgment, or tradeoffs?
- What event counts as meaningful inbound: a reader, a technical collaborator, a buyer conversation, a pilot request, or revenue?
- How long will inbound run before outbound begins?
- Would this demo still be worth its cost if it generated legitimacy but no enterprise demand?
- When Niyant says “computer-use NAP,” does he mean exact semantic destination routing, broader future-action trajectories, or the live Tab interaction?
- Does correct personal history improve held-out semantic routing beyond screen-only context and simple source-transition habits?
