# Public proof can establish builder legitimacy without proving enterprise demand

## Claim

A rigorous public demo can show that a team understands a hard problem and can execute. It may earn attention or a conversation that the team could not get with a pitch alone. It does not show that an enterprise has the problem often enough, can deploy the solution, or will pay for it.

Builder legitimacy, technical efficacy, and market demand are separate outputs.

## Why It Matters

Niyant's current sequence relies on public demos, writing, a landing page, and a pricing page to accumulate legitimacy and attract inbound before doing outbound enterprise outreach. That may be a sensible way to overcome an access problem.

The risk is evidence substitution. A polished demo can feel like market progress because it produces attention. Inbound can feel like demand because someone took a meeting. Neither answers whether the product improves a real workflow under enterprise constraints.

Keeping the outputs separate preserves the value of an honest result:

- A failed technical hypothesis can still demonstrate rigorous execution.
- A successful personal demo can establish a narrow capability without implying enterprise transfer.
- Inbound can justify a conversation without being counted as adoption.

## Evidence

In the July 21 Slack exchange, Niyant said he needs to show the ability to solve the problem publicly to have a chance with sales. He does not consider enterprise outreach a short-term goal. His plan is to build legitimacy through public artifacts and collect inbound, using outbound only if the inbound strategy set fails.

## Implications

- Define the intended audience and claim for each public artifact.
- Record whether inbound asks about the demonstrated capability, a different problem, investment, collaboration, or employment.
- Do not count a pricing-page visit, compliment, meeting, or pilot discussion as willingness to pay.
- Preserve a demo's proof-of-work value even when the technical or market hypothesis fails.

## Counterpoints / Uncertainty

- In markets where buyers will not engage without proof, a substantial demo may be the cheapest route to credible discovery.
- Some inbound signals are stronger than others. A buyer who supplies data and commits workflow time is more informative than a general expression of interest.
- The right evidence sequence depends on the cost of the demo and the cost of reaching buyers directly.
