# USD.AI differentiates on standardization and distribution, not residual valuation

## Claim

Estimating GPU residual value is not USD.AI's differentiated insight. Any competent lender taking GPUs as collateral must estimate current value, depreciation, and recovery. Large lenders already combine those marks with offtake credit, amortization, collateral controls, and downside recovery analysis.

USD.AI currently uses most of the same economic ingredients. Its real product claim is that it can package them more cheaply and repeatedly: isolate hardware and project cash flow from the operator's broader corporate credit, use standardized nonrecourse documents and collateral controls, originate loans too small for bespoke private-credit economics, and recycle the exposure into an onchain pool funded by sUSD.AI depositors.

Its long-term claim is more ambitious and more differentiated: replace single-operator loans with a liquid, diversified pool of machine-level GPU credit. That is a capital-markets and distribution thesis, not a superior-residual-model thesis.

## What Is Standard

- Lenders estimate GPU residual and liquidation value before setting advance rates.
- Hardware provides recoverable collateral while offtake or rental income services the loan.
- Bankruptcy-remote or nonrecourse project structures, cash sweeps, insurance, and collateral monitoring already exist in equipment and infrastructure finance.
- Stronger customer credit earns better terms. USD.AI's own 7-15% pricing ladder and contract-heavy public pipeline follow this market logic.

## What Is Different Today

- **Deal economics:** Connor says private-credit funds need roughly $500M deals because bespoke diligence and legal work make $30-50M loans unattractive; USD.AI claims one process from roughly $300K to hundreds of millions.
- **Underwriting boundary:** it tries to remove the operator's speculative equity value and broader corporate balance sheet from the credit decision, focusing on clean hardware title, recovery value, and project cash flow.
- **Funding and distribution:** deposits continuously fund a pooled loan book, while sUSD.AI gives investors a tradable claim without waiting years for a conventional ABS issuance.
- **Risk appetite:** it can bridge a pre-offtake build at a higher rate when a conventional lender might decline, although its visible mature book still overwhelmingly has contracts.

## Honest Read

Today, USD.AI looks less like a new theory of lending and more like tech-enabled, crypto-funded equipment or project finance for a segment that bespoke private credit serves inefficiently. That can be a real business, but it is differentiation through process, capital source, deal size, and speed rather than through recognizing that GPUs have residual value.

The genuinely novel part remains prospective: machine-level origination plus a liquid diversified GPU-loan pool that becomes a market benchmark. It is unproven whether the onchain wrapper lowers funding and transaction costs enough, whether the pool develops real secondary liquidity, and whether diversification survives the correlated risk that compute revenue and GPU values can fall together.

## Evidence

- Connor Moore, Messari, ~5:06-9:31: banks avoid servers, private credit favors very large bespoke transactions, and USD.AI proposes standardized asset-level underwriting funded through a yield token.
- Connor Moore, ~20:49-23:51: the claimed onchain advantage is immediate aggregation, tradability, transparent pricing, and avoiding a conventional ABS process that can take years.
- Connor Moore, ~34:37-36:03: USD.AI says it can use boilerplate nonrecourse documents from roughly $300K through hundreds of millions, including the $30-50M segment that is uneconomic for large private-credit teams.
- David Choi, Touchcraft, ~9:15-15:06: the end state is a diversified security across GPUs and customers rather than another CoreWeave or Lambda single-name loan.
- Bernie Margulies, 2026-06-16: lenders already estimate residual value but treat it mainly as worst-case recovery while focusing on offtake.
- USD.AI public book, 2026-07-17: eight of nine upcoming loans were contract-backed, showing that current execution remains much closer to conventional contract-plus-collateral finance than the end-state rhetoric.

## Tests

- Does USD.AI close comparable loans faster and with lower legal and diligence costs than private credit?
- After all fees and token incentives, is its borrower cost of capital lower for the same risk?
- Do no-offtake loans produce recoveries that validate collateral-first underwriting through a down cycle?
- Does sUSD.AI develop durable secondary liquidity and market-based credit pricing without subsidies?
- Does the loan pool become diversified across operators and customers, or remain a concentrated contract-backed book?
