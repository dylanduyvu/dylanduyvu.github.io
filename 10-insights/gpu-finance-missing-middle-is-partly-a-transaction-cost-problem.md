# GPU finance's missing middle is partly a transaction-cost problem

## Claim

The financing gap around mid-sized GPU clusters may exist partly because conventional private-credit loans are too expensive to manufacture, not only because operators are hard to underwrite.

Connor Moore says USD.AI's typical cluster is $30-50 million, while bespoke private-credit execution carries enough legal cost and document complexity to push traditional funds toward roughly $500 million tickets. USD.AI's proposed wedge is standardized, nonrecourse, asset-secured execution with visible committed liquidity, available from $300 thousand upward.

On this account, the missing middle is a market-plumbing problem before it is a verification-product problem.

## Why It Matters

This is a competing explanation for the operator-history thesis. A portable record cannot unlock a deal if the lender's fixed cost makes the ticket uneconomic even after the risk is understood.

It also points toward a different product shape: standardized documents, financing capacity, faster closing, and predictable terms may be more valuable than another diligence artifact.

## Evidence

- Connor places the typical USD.AI borrower cluster at $30-50 million.
- He says private credit wants roughly $500 million transactions because bespoke legal work is too costly for smaller loans. He gives inconsistent cost figures in the same episode, first "hundreds of thousands" and later $2 million, so the direction is more reliable than the exact number.
- He describes traditional structures as hundred-page master lease agreements with warrants and deliberate complexity.
- USD.AI says it can lend from $300 thousand to hundreds of millions using boilerplate, nonrecourse documents secured by the hardware.
- The borrower can see available protocol capital and terms before placing a purchase order, making execution certainty part of the product.

## Implications

- Test closing time, legal expense, documentation burden, and certainty of funds directly with operators and lenders. These may rank above verification.
- A middle-market product may need to own or aggregate capital, not merely sell data into someone else's credit process.
- Operator evidence matters commercially only if it changes eligibility, reserves, insurance, or terms inside a standardized financing channel.
- USD.AI's front end may look automated while regional originators still perform expert credit work. Standardization can compress execution cost without eliminating underwriting.

## Counterpoints / Uncertainty

- This is one founder describing his own product against competitors, so the framing is promotional.
- Private-credit funds do make sub-$500 million loans; Connor's threshold should not be treated as a universal market minimum.
- USD.AI may relocate costs into protocol governance, insurance, first-loss capital, and pooled liquidity rather than eliminate them.
- The planned hub-and-spoke network of regional credit funds reintroduces human underwriting and originator-quality risk.
- Fixed transaction cost and missing information can coexist. The claim is that transaction execution is a competing bottleneck, not the only one.
