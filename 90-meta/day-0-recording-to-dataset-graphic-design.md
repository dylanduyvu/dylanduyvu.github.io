# Day 0 article: recording-to-dataset graphic design

## Purpose

Explain the article's central distinction in one glance:

> continuous recording → missing conversion → usable navigation record

The graphic should teach the gap between captured evidence and the dataset. It should not decorate the article, repeat the Arc-to-Codex example, or foreground the three-day setup delay.

## Placement

Insert the graphic in `20-syntheses/day-0-took-three-days.md` immediately after the five conversion steps in `Recording captured evidence, not dataset records`.

## Content

Use three left-to-right stages:

1. **Continuous evidence:** screens, clicks and keys, app and window events, browser events, and both monitors.
2. **Missing conversion:** find the navigation moment, recover the prior state, join the evidence, and name the destination.
3. **Navigation record:** what was on Dylan's screens before the move → the exact place he went next.

Keep the middle stage shorter than the article's numbered list. The graphic summarizes the relationship instead of reproducing every sentence.

## Visual direction

- 1600 × 800 PNG for Quartz and direct Substack upload.
- Off-white background, near-black text, muted rust accent for the conversion layer, and muted green for the completed record.
- Plain sans-serif typography.
- No product logos, decorative illustration, gradients, or em dashes.
- Clear at mobile width and legible when viewed at half size.

## Accessibility

Use this alt text:

> Continuous screen and input evidence must pass through a conversion layer that identifies navigation moments, recovers prior screen state, joins both monitors, and names the destination before it becomes a chronological navigation record.
