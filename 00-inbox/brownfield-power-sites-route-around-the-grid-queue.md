# Hunch: brownfield power sites are how operators route around the grid queue

Rough pattern, n~3, not yet earned an insight note.

The grid interconnection queue is the slowest, most fatal layer of the datacenter delay stack (LBNL: only ~13% of 2000-2019 queue entrants ever reached operation). The operators winning the delivery race seem to be the ones who bypass the queue entirely by acquiring or improvising legacy power:

- TeraWulf's Justified Data campus (2026-07-06 Anthropic lease): a former Century Aluminum smelter, chosen explicitly for the bulk power transmission infrastructure the smelter left behind.
- xAI Colossus: 122 days to 100k GPUs by running gas turbines instead of waiting on the grid (now facing EPA exposure after the loophole closed Jan 2026).
- Crusoe's whole heritage: stranded/flared energy converted to compute.
- (Adjacent: the ex-bitcoin-miner class generally - TeraWulf, Crusoe, Hive - pivoted to AI partly because they already own power assets.)

If real, the implications are underwriting-relevant: power provenance (owned brownfield vs queue-dependent) may be one of the strongest observable predictors of delivery risk, i.e. a diligence variable a lender could score today without any new verification infrastructure. It would also mean legacy industrial sites with heavy power infrastructure (smelters, foundries, paper mills) are quietly becoming a scarce financial asset class.

Watch for: more smelter/industrial-site acquisitions by operators; whether lenders start pricing power provenance; whether queue-dependent projects (the Sightline 26%) skew the delay stats while brownfield projects deliver on time.

Promote to 10-insights if: a lender or insurer confirms they differentiate on power provenance, or 2-3 more brownfield acquisitions land, or delay data splits cleanly along this line.
