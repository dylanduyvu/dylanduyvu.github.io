## The prompt (paste as is)

Objective: Build a contact list of ~50 people at lending firms that finance GPU / AI-compute operators in the $5M-$100M loan range, so I can run short structured interviews about how they underwrite unproven operators. I am testing one hypothesis: whether a verified operator delivery track record would change loan eligibility or financing terms. I need the people who actually run these deals and see repeat borrowers. This is wave three: a first wave of 46 went out 2026-07-07 and a second of 19 on 2026-07-14. Exclude every firm from both waves (lists attached).

Who qualifies (the ICP):

1. Boutique private credit and specialty lending firms doing GPU-backed or equipment-backed loans to small and mid-size compute operators (neoclouds, GPU clouds, datacenter operators, ex-crypto miners pivoting to AI). Typical deal size $5M-$100M, yields roughly 10-15%.
2. Equipment finance and leasing companies active in GPU / AI hardware (term loans, FMV leases, sale-leasebacks).
3. Venture debt and specialty finance shops with AI infrastructure exposure.
4. Credit funds and family offices that PARTICIPATE in GPU facilities arranged by others (club deals, slices of private facilities), even if they never originate. Their view of what travels in a lender pack is part of what I'm studying.
5. Crypto-native or on-chain GPU credit protocols and funds. This category is thin (a handful of real players); cap it at 5 rows, and exclude USD.AI / Permian Labs and Silicon Network (existing relationships).
6. Family offices or small funds publicly known to lend against GPUs or compute contracts.
7. Digital-infrastructure credit teams inside mid-size asset managers, only if they do sub-$100M operator-level deals.

Who to exclude: every firm on the attached wave-one and wave-two lists; USD.AI / Permian Labs, SemiAnalysis, American Compute, Barkr, Silicon Network, Refinery (existing relationships); mega private credit at the investment-grade tier (Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, PIMCO-scale teams doing $1B+ deals with anchor tenants); banks; pure equity/VC investors; insurance and residual-value firms; brokers and placement agents; anyone whose only GPU exposure is public bonds.

Target roles, in priority order:
1. Head of originations, and VP / director / principal / SVP in originations or portfolio management covering equipment finance, digital infrastructure, or datacenter credit. This is the priority layer: the people who run deals and see repeat borrowers.
2. Founder or managing partner, but only at firms small enough (roughly under 20 people) that they run deals themselves.
3. Chief credit officer or head of underwriting.
Avoid pure C-suite at larger firms (they don't run deals) and avoid analysts/associates everywhere (they can't speak to outsiders).

How to find them (suggested sources): lenders named in disclosed GPU financing deals 2024-2026 (press releases, DataCenterDynamics and datacenter trade press financing coverage, articles on neocloud debt); members of NVIDIA's preferred or approved funder ecosystem named in trade coverage; lenders named in Google or AMD backstopped-deal coverage; firms like Forum Markets and Digital Alpha as seed examples of the category; speaker lists from datacenter and digital-infrastructure finance conferences (The Tech Capital International Finance Forum) and equipment finance events (ELFA); podcast and webinar guests discussing GPU-backed lending, including crypto-credit podcasts, where qualified lenders often surface first; LinkedIn titles combining credit/originations/underwriting with GPU, AI infrastructure, datacenter, or equipment finance; UCC filings or trade coverage naming lenders to small GPU operators.

Output format: a table with columns: name, title, seniority layer (deal-runner / founder-operator / credit-officer), firm, firm type (from the 7 categories), originator or participant (if determinable), email, email confidence (verified / pattern-inferred / guessed), LinkedIn URL, evidence of GPU or compute lending activity (one line with a source link), deal size range if known, one-line relevance note.

Quality bars: aim for 50 qualified contacts (gather 65-70 raw and cut the weakest). Email accuracy matters more than volume: wave one had 15 delivery failures out of 46, which materially weakened the experiment, so verify addresses hard and label confidence honestly per row. No more than 3 contacts per firm; prioritize breadth of firms over depth per firm. US-first, but include qualifying non-US firms. Flag any contact where the firm may have already exited GPU lending.
