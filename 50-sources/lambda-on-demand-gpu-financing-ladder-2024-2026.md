## 2024 merchant facility

Lambda announced a special-purpose GPU financing vehicle of up to $500 million led by Macquarie, with Industrial Development Funding participating. The stated use was expansion of Lambda's on-demand cloud offering.

Lambda described the structure as secured by the GPUs and supported by the cash flow those GPUs generated. It explicitly said the facility allowed thousands of users to rent capacity without signing long-term contracts. The Wall Street Journal separately described the vehicle as funding on-demand cloud services, with GPU rental and cloud-service cash flows maintaining the facility.

This is direct public evidence that institutional merchant GPU credit existed by April 2024. It is a counterexample to any claim that lenders categorically require named long-term offtake.

The public announcement did not disclose the interest rate, advance rate, term, amortization, reserves, covenants, utilization assumptions, customer-concentration limits, or expected re-rental time. Lambda already had more than 100,000 customer sign-ups and had operated since 2012, so the facility does not establish that an unproven operator could obtain the same structure.

## Later facilities

In August 2025, Lambda closed a $275 million syndicated senior secured credit facility led by J.P. Morgan, with Citi, MUFG, Credit Agricole, and other lenders. The release described a flexible facility that could grow with the business. It did not disclose whether the collateral package or repayment model matched the 2024 merchant vehicle.

In May 2026, Lambda upsized that facility to $1 billion. The release credited Lambda's operating track record and said the syndicate had confidence in the company's contracted revenue base and business model. Again, detailed terms and the mix of contracted versus merchant cash flow were not disclosed.

The ladder shows merchant financing followed by broader syndicated credit as Lambda seasoned. It does not prove the 2024 underwriting model became portable to other operators. The later references to track record and contracted revenue could mean Lambda graduated into corporate or contract-supported credit rather than standardizing merchant asset-level lending.

## Implications for the second-customer thesis

- Merchant GPU credit is possible at institutional size. Lambda closed the clean public counterexample.
- The missing item is not proof of possibility. It is a disclosed and repeatable underwriting model that another operator can use.
- The 2024 facility is the best target for primary research. Macquarie, IDF, Lambda, and Lazard may be able to explain how the lender sized utilization risk and principal beyond current customer commitments.
- The later ladder may show operator track record converting a bespoke merchant facility into broad syndicated credit, but the public releases do not expose enough terms to establish the mechanism.
