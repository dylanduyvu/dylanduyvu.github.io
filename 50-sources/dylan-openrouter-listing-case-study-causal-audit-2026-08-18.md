# Dylan: The OpenRouter case studies do not support the pre-listing causal claim

## Context

Dylan audited the provider-listing report and corrected its central causal claim. The official process and post-listing day-0 evidence survive. The case evidence does not show that day-0 shipping caused provider acceptance.

## Raw Context

TL;DR:

Yes. The central causal claim of the report is unsupported. You're right to be pissed. What actually survives: (1) the official process — solid, documented; (2) day-0 shipping as a post-listing growth tactic — well documented; (3) the honest finding underneath it all: nobody has ever published how they actually got listed. The report found a void, and instead of reporting the void, it filled it with a plausible story and put case-study names on it.

Here's the straight accounting. The report had two real ingredients: OpenRouter's own quote ("priority is given to providers that fill gaps") and a pile of LinkedIn posts celebrating day-0 launches. It welded them into "day-0 is the application hook." That weld is inference. The case studies only ever show day-0 after listing. The report's own caveats section even says the approval mechanics are "almost never published" — and then the headline asserts them anyway. It contradicted itself, and the pass caught it.

So relabel the report honestly and it becomes: a good report on how to grow once listed, plus proof that the pre-listing process is a public black box.

Two things follow from that, and they're both actually good for you:

The void is your article. You set out to write "the unwritten process of getting listed on OpenRouter." The research just proved the process is genuinely unwritten — no provider has published it. Your Thursday article isn't competing with anything. And the BD logic gets stronger: the team drowning in a provider backlog would notice the first real guide.

The strategy barely changes — only its label does. "Ship a gap-filling model, then apply" is still the best available bet. But it now rests on OpenRouter's stated priority (solid, quotable) rather than on case evidence (nonexistent). It's a hypothesis. Which means if you run it and document it, you become the only public case study — and honestly, "we applied, here's what actually happened" is a better article than the one you planned.
