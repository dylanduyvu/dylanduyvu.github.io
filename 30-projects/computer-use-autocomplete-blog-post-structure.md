## The spine, 9 beats

1. Computer use is valuable: it removes the friction between intent and machine.
2. Capability is arriving faster than **trust** (horror stories).
3. Industry patches trust on (permissioning). Better: design it in, human tight in the loop.
4. One different direction: tighten the loop between human and machine actions. **Tab autocomplete for computer use** is the tightest version.
5. That design needs a system, laid out once up front: a recorder that captures and structures your work, a predictor that reads the recording and knows you, the pill and its keys, and the hands that click. Everything after details a part of it.
6. The hard part is the predictor. Unlike text autocomplete, it can't read intent off the artifact, so it has to **know you**: the intent lives in the person, not the screen. Pretraining subsidizes part of this, far less than it does for code. What remains climbs a ladder of **capture debt**: the personal signal Cursor needs ships with the work (your repo), Cotypist's is scattered across your past text, and most of ours was never recorded at all.
7. For each layer, we looked for something to adopt before building. The executors are locked to their makers' own apps. The models actually trained on computer actions are private. The recorders that exist can't produce training-grade work history. Only the pill layer was fully buildable. (Swept 2026-08-14 per the term decision; superseded wording: "verified work history.")
8. So we built the missing pieces ourselves. They kept breaking, and the fixing ate the project: the product got one clean afternoon of real use, so the idea never got a fair test. Our one clean experiment caps hopes anyway: a top model with my full history only ever predicted my habits, nothing new. Other teams chasing this ship everything except the part that clicks; we think they hit the same wall.
9. Both failure threads point at the same **two missing pieces**: a recorder for your own work history (capture now commoditizing, the training-grade half still missing), and a model trained on computer actions (still private). Text autocomplete got both free: the document is the record, and the models were pretrained on documents. (Amended 2026-08-14 per the approved candidate below.)

**Frame decision (2026-08-14, per Dylan): the optimistic gradient.** Three parts moved toward adopt or package since the kill: interaction was always buildable, Cua Driver moved the executor, and Computer History moved capture to copy-and-normalize at research grade, shipped the day we stopped. The predictor did not move. The post says the arrival plainly. Bottleneck 2 and the sec 8 rings stay as the reasons pure packaging would overclaim. Ending stays a hypothesis per the style rule.

**Candidate beat 9 amendment (2026-08-14, needs Dylan's audit alongside the pending beats 5 and 6 audit):** "...a recorder for your own work history (capture now commoditizing, the training-grade half still missing), and a model trained on computer actions (still private)." **(Audit closed 2026-08-14 under Dylan's best-decisions delegation: beats 5 and 6 approved as reworded, beat 7 swept, beat 9 amendment applied above.)**

### 6. Why the predictor has to know you (was sec 5; renumbered in the 2026-08-13 restructure, content unchanged)

**The argument in five lines (assertive form restored 2026-08-13; the audits fixed wording, the flags live in the derivation below). Everything below it is derivation, flags, and objection history: keep for drafting defense, safe to skip.**

1. To predict a next step, something has to carry the goal. Usually the goal itself; sometimes a proxy that embodies it, like habit.
2. The goal gets onto the record three ways: you act it out (weak signal per step), you write it down (strong signal), or the world hands it to you (an event). Some of it never arrives.
3. The behavioral record comes down to two numbers: goal per step, and steps per goal. Words carry a lot and come in hundreds. Navigation actions carry little and come in a handful.
4. Text autocomplete lives downstream of disclosure: by the time it fires, the goal is on the page. Navigation autocomplete lives upstream: it fires at the boundary, before the acting has said anything.
5. So the gap closes from elsewhere: repetition, declarations, events, or asking. A stronger model shrinks how much goal you need. What never reaches any record and won't be told stays unpredictable for any system. That is the hard limit.

**Why the shared-symbols objection fails (added 2026-08-13):**

- Nobody "writes the same." Unconditionally, writing is as personal as computer use. The invariance is conditional: given the prefix, continuations converge. So shared letters and words are not the explanation, and computer use shares its symbols too (everyone's Gmail has the same buttons).
- The real asymmetry: **writing states intent; acting implies it, coarsely and late.**
  - Actions do disclose. Clicking Gmail rules out YouTube-shaped goals. But that is P(action | goal): given the goal, the action converges.
  - The predictor needs the other direction, P(goal | record). That diverges: Gmail hosts dozens of goals.
  - Don't over-prove it: the predictor only needs the goal's next destination, not the goal. Many goals collapsing into one destination can make prediction easy.
  - Net claim: prediction works where the record concentrates the next destination (habit). It fails where the destination spreads (novel).
- **Disclosure lags prediction by one step.** The click that reveals the new intent is the click you had to predict.
  - Text: one intent emits many tokens. Most predictions come after disclosure.
  - Computer: one intent emits few actions. More predictions land at boundaries, before disclosure. (Magnitudes unmeasured.)
- **The compressed version:** text autocomplete sells within-intent completion, where intent is already disclosed. Action autocomplete sells boundary crossing, where it is not.
  - Our targets were mostly boundaries. Not all: V3 had prompt-submit twice, both conditions missed both. Within-run predictability is assumed, not shown.
  - "Value concentrates at the boundary" is a design argument, not a measurement.
- Math is the extreme of conditional convergence (logic is the tightest shared constraint). The ladder: math > code > prose > computer use, ordered by how much the visible record constrains the next step.
- Habit is the footnote our data wrote: repetition permanently discloses a standing intent into the record, and that disclosed portion is exactly and only what V5 recovered. Fresh boundaries got zero. Moment detection and the Codex-completion trigger are the same idea from the other side: moments where the world has just disclosed the intent shift for you.
- **Substitutability, co-primary for prose.**
  - Given a long prose prefix, continuations diverge in content. Text autocomplete survives because many continuations are acceptable to the author.
  - Actions are exact: one right thread, one right control. Near misses are worthless.
  - Convergence carries math and code. Substitutability carries prose. Our exact-versus-semantic scoring fights were this difference biting.
- Symmetry check: writing also needs the person at the blank page; the prefix warms it up within a paragraph. Acting warms up within a run and resets at every boundary. Every boundary is a fresh blank page.

Why ours must know you. Decompose the prediction error into three terms (superseded the flat "three inversions" list, 2026-08-13):

1. **Missing priors.** What screens afford, how workflows typically unfold. This IS a data-scale problem: solvable by pretraining (Gap 2). Explains part of our measured zeros; untestable from outside a lab because the models are private.
2. **Missing person-signal.** Same screen, different goals. Structural, NOT a scale problem: other people's data teaches the average person's next action, the population marginal. Your conditional needs your bits in the input. Scale sharpens a distribution; it cannot collapse ambiguity caused by a hidden variable. Text at scale works because, given the prefix, continuations converge or many are acceptable; given a screen alone, neither holds.
3. **Possibly irreducible.** The goal that never touches the machine. This is the legibility hypothesis, parked in sec 9.

**The subsidy spectrum (added 2026-08-13):**

- In every domain, the next step splits into a shared part and a personal residual. Pretraining plus visible context pays for the shared part. Only your signal pays for the residual.
- Code has a double advantage: the residual is small, and it sits in one dense, intent-encoding artifact (the repo) that tooling already retrieves.
- Computer use has the double disadvantage: the residual is large, and it has no such artifact. It splits by location:
  - On the record already, via habit leakage. Small. Exactly what V5 recovered.
  - Declared already: calendars, task lists, message threads, Codex task titles. High-bit, machine-readable, under-used by our packet beyond the task catalog. The recovered V5 destination was a user-written task title; the one correct cross-app proposal named a declared object. The declared-goal experiment condition was designed July 23 and never run.
  - Recoverable, if a real recorder existed. Gap 1's target. Size unknown.
  - In the head only, never touching the machine. The legibility hypothesis. Size unknown. Elicitation (asking) can convert some of this into record.
- This split maps onto the post: habit (sec 8), declarations and recorder (Gap 1), legibility (sec 9).
- **The head-only residual is universal (added after Dylan's objection, 2026-08-13):** code intent is head-born too. Cursor never predicts your next feature or next file; it predicts the continuation of intent you have already typed. Text autocomplete chose a target downstream of disclosure. Ours sat upstream, at boundaries. The remaining difference is drain rate: code forces intent through the keyboard into the artifact, densely, in intent vocabulary. Computer use completes an intent with a few clicks that barely encode it.
- Measured hint of the split: state-only got action type 9/10 in V5 but exact destination 0/10. Shared priors buy the type. Personal signal buys the destination.
- How much a computer-use pretrained model shrinks the residual: unmeasured, because Gap 2 is private. Hypothesis, not finding.
- The ladder restated: math > code > prose > computer use is a ranking of how much the shared subsidy covers.

**The terminus (2026-08-13): no difference in kind, two parameters.**

- After all objections collapsed the dichotomies, what survives: one structure, two parameters of the behavioral channel (declarations and events sit alongside, per line 2 of the five lines).
  - Bits of intent per step: how much of the goal each emitted unit writes into the record. A word: high. A click: low.
  - Steps per intent: how long the record accumulates before the goal changes. Code: hundreds of tokens. Navigation: a handful of actions.
- Text is high times many. Computer-use navigation is low times few.
- Prior framings are corollaries, kept above as the path here: the prefix is accumulated high-bit serialization; the artifact-carries-intent claim is the limit of parameter one; boundary difficulty is the limit of parameter two; habit is the stationary-goal shortcut that skips disclosure; moment detection is the world injecting intent bits.
- Why this framing is stronger: a continuum cannot be punctured like a dichotomy, and it predicts where computer-use prediction should work (high-bit, long-run flows) and where it should not (sparse navigation).
- Limitation: neither parameter was measured in our data. Estimable in principle from the ledger; not done.

The entry-point formulation (early form, partly superseded: the channel is thin, not deleted, and declarations and events are channels too; keep only as the draft's opening intuition):

> Every next-step predictor needs a goal channel. Writing supplies it as the prefix. Agents get it as the typed instruction. Ambient autocomplete deletes both, so the only channel left is **the person**. Personalization isn't a feature choice; it's forced by the information structure of the task. The personal recorder manufactures for actions what the document gives text for free.
